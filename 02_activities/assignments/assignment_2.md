#### Good Visualization
Source: https://blog.hubspot.com/hs-fs/hubfs/Screen%20Shot%202020-09-02%20at%2010.27.59%20PM.png?width=2250&name=Screen%20Shot%202020-09-02%20at%2010.27.59%20PM.png
    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
      ```
      This is a good visualization as it follows many of the principles we discussed in class such as Gestalt principles, cognitive load, and Kennedy et al. guidelines. To begin with, this graph has a descriptive title and axis labels that make it clear what data is being displayed. In terms of cognitive load, this requires minimal effort by the user to interpret. Firstly, it uses a familiar chart type and accurate interpretation with absolute values. As well, aesthetically the grid lines make it very easy to determine these absolute values without too much thinking. Furthermore, this is a very consise graphic with not a lot of visual elements. It also follows most of Kennedy et al.'s guidelines with a 2-D image, clean layout, and use of solely geometric shapes and lines. This plot also follows the Gestalt principles in the way the data is plotted; For example, the proximity of data points suggests a trend/clustering and the continuity of the scatter plot in an upwards trend helps guide the viewer's eye. 
      ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
      One thing that could be added to this chart is the inclusion of the data source at the bottom of the image, as recommended by Kennedy et al. Citing the data source helps for reproducibility sake and also helps people trust the data visualization more. While the creator included units on the Y-axis, they forgot to do the same on the X-axis. The axis is labelled "Ground living area;" While this generally is enough to get the main points across regardless of units, the inclusion of units makes the graphic more concise and gives a more accurate interpretation. Overall, these minimal fixes will turn this good visualization into a great one!
      ```

#### Bad Visualization
Source: https://dribbble.com/shots/19821729-Bar-Chart
    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
      ```
      This was classified as a bad visualization since it goes against many of the keys for "good" visualization that we discussed in class. To begin with, the lack of axis labels and titles makes this chart difficult to interpret right off the bat. In fact, I actually can't even tell what the purpose of this chart is. The lack of Y-axis also makes this difficult to interpret or compare between months; this draws upon the theory of cognitive load as discussed in lecture. Our working memory is limited, so it helps to decrease cognitive load when possible. This chart uses approximate interpretation of area / relational values instead of absolute. Also along the lines of cognitive load, this is a very detailed composition with many visual elements. More specifically, the graphic of the person takes away from the actual data-based visualization. The colours of the categorical variable (Patterns/solids) also changes in alignment with the person's skin tone which makes the person really have to think about interpretation. Similarly, this graphic of the woman does not follow the guidelines set out by Kennedy et al. which say to include only geometric lines and shapes. Aesthetically, the random geometric shapes (squares/rectangles) in blue are confusing since they have nothing to do with the actual data and overlap with the bars; this violates the Gestalt principles and makes it confusing as to which elements are data versus decoration.
      ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
      This visualization could be improved by changing the graphic of the woman in such a way that it does not impede the real data of the chart or removing non-data elements entirely. Trying to incorporate more of Kennedey et al.'s recommendations such as using clean layouts and geometric shapes/lines could be beneficial here. One example of this could be ensuring the bars are consistent colours as outlined in the legend. Adding Y-axis labels would provide an opportunity to have a more accurate intepretation (with absolute values) and making this graphic more concise would decrease the cognitive load for the viewer.
      ```
